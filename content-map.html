Reading Guide and Concept Map

9 min read·4 sections
On this page
Book SpineConcept MapCross-Referenced Concept ClustersPage-by-Page Map

This page is the book-level map. It explains the main spine of the book, where each concept is introduced, and where the same idea is revisited from an implementation, architecture, enterprise, or reference angle. Repetition is intentional when it helps a reader move from first intuition to production practice.

How to use this map For each major concept, start with the primary chapter, then follow the related chapters when you want a second angle: a beginner introduction, an applied implementation, an enterprise design pattern, or a reference edition.

Book Spine

Part Primary purpose How to read it
Part I Enterprise AI delivery lifecycle: intake, kickoff, HLD, architecture review, LLD, sprint planning, provisioning, and scale. Read as a process story. Repeated roles, artifacts, and gates are intentional lifecycle continuity.
Part II Backend runtime, HTTP/FastAPI mechanics, Pydantic data contracts, authentication, identity, tenancy, workload identity, external integrations, and governance. Read in four blocks: runtime basics, Pydantic data boundaries, auth identity, then tenancy/IAM/GCP implementation.
Part III Agent protocol integration: MCP fundamentals, remote/auth/enterprise patterns, 2025-to-2026 evolution, A2A 1.0, JS/TS, and LangChain/LangGraph/Flowise. Read the MCP chapters together, then A2A as the separate agent-to-agent delegation protocol.
Part IV Classical ML foundations: PyTorch, geometry, regression, classification, trees, bagging, boosting, and XGBoost. Read before Part V. Regression and classification share the same training workflow but differ in target, output, and loss.
Part V Deep learning and LLM track: FFNs, CNNs, forecasting, RNNs, Transformers, fine-tuning, and alignment. Read by cluster: FFN, CNN, sequence models, then LLMs.
Part VI Supporting references: interview talk track and print-oriented classification source. Use as review material or a printable companion.

End-user identity, tenant isolation, IAM, AD/Workspace groups, and workload identity Part II Ch. 12 Ch. 13 applies the same three-lane model to AD groups and bootstrap policy. Ch. 14 implements it on GCP. Part I Ch. 6-7 mention it only at architecture-decision level.

A2A 1.0 in Ch. 5 is deliberately separate: inter-agent delegation, not host-to-tool integration.
Classical ML workflow: features, target, model, loss, optimizer, fit/predict/score Part IV Ch. 2 Ch. 3 instantiates the workflow for numeric regression. Ch. 4 instantiates it for classification with logits, sigmoid/softmax, and accuracy. Ch. 5 branches to trees and ensembles for tabular baselines.
Feed-forward neural networks, neurons, activations, backprop, batching, and debugging Part V Ch. 1 and Ch. 2 Ch. 3 is the optional single-chapter reference edition. It repeats the same concepts in one article for print/reference use.
CNN convolution, feature maps, pooling, implementation, dropout, and train/eval behavior Part V Ch. 4 through Ch. 6 Ch. 7 expands the short dropout and train/eval section from Ch. 6 into a full conceptual and coding deep dive.
Sequence forecasting, autoregressive windows, leakage, recursion, RNNs, and Transformers Part V Ch. 8 Ch. 9 implements the autoregressive linear baseline. Ch. 10 replaces a fixed window with recurrent state, builds from vanilla RNN into GRU/LSTM gates, then uses encoder-decoder translation to show tokens, embeddings, padding, teacher forcing, and autoregressive decoding. Ch. 11 builds from that translation model into cross-attention, self-attention, causal masking, and GPT generation.
LLM adaptation, SFT, PEFT, LoRA/QLoRA, DPO/RLHF, ORPO, KTO, GRPO, RLAIF Part V Ch. 12 for adaptation; Ch. 13 for alignment comparison. Ch. 12 introduces SFT/PEFT and first-generation preference methods. Ch. 13 deliberately repeats the DPO/RLHF baseline only to compare newer alignment methods in one decision framework.
